# Feature C: privacy and consent checklist

Student project. FAKE and VOLUNTEER data only. Not for real residents.

## 1. What face data we will collect
- Only from adult student volunteers who agree, and from public datasets.
- Not from residents, staff or any real care setting.
- Data collected: a photo or short video of the volunteer's face, taken for testing.
- Until the lecturers answer Question 3, we use only our own photos.
- We collect nothing else about the volunteer (no health information, no ID).

## 2. Consent
- Each volunteer signs a one-page consent form in plain words before we take any photo.
- The form says: what we collect, why (a student project to test a tool), how long we keep it, and who can see it.
- Volunteers can say no, and can change their mind at any time. If they withdraw, we delete their data.
- Nobody is pressured to take part. Taking part has no effect on their marks or anything else.

## 3. Storage and processing
- The check runs on the laptop itself (on-device). Images are not sent to any online service.
- The tool does not save the image. It only reads face points (landmarks) and then discards the picture.
- Any test photo we must keep is stored on one team laptop, in a folder that is not committed to GitHub.
- Test photos are deleted at the end of the project (Week 8 or when Studio 6 ends), or earlier if the volunteer asks.
- Only the three team members can see them.

## 4. What the tool never does
- It never names a disease or gives a diagnosis.
- It only flags visible cues (for example pain or distress, facial asymmetry, reduced expression) for a nurse to look at.
- A nurse always decides what the cue means and what to do.
- It never identifies who a person is.
- It is a student prototype and is not a medical device.

## 5. NZ rules to check
- Privacy Act 2020: collect only what we need, tell people why, keep it safe, keep it no longer than needed, let people see and correct it.
- Health Information Privacy Code 2020: applies if this ever handles real health information. Not needed for our fake and volunteer data, but a real product would need it.
- Biometric Processing Privacy Code 2025: face analysis is biometric processing. The Privacy Commissioner's overview says health information should not be detected from biometrics unless the person has specifically authorised it. This is why the tool flags visible cues only and names no condition.
- Note: we have read summaries only, not the full text of the Code. To confirm with the lecturers.
